# Season 2 — Dot-Com & Mobile Wars (planning draft)

> **Status:** Story/character proposal for review. Season 1 `bible/story_bible.md`, approved images and `characters/*/continuity.yaml` remain the authority for established heroines. No existing character or artwork is overwritten.

## Premise

**Act I · Dial-Up Metropolis (1995–2002)** asks who controls access, discovery, and the physical roads of the web. **Act II · Pocket Kingdom (1999–2012)** asks who controls the handset, wireless link, operating system and app distribution. Present-day framing scenes may connect the prequel to the Age of Artificial Intelligence. Corporate metaphors are fictional; timelines are historical anchors, not events performed by literal corporate people.

## Timeline and fact / fiction boundary

| Era | Historical anchor (sources below) | Fictional dramatization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1994–1996 | Yahoo emerged as a web directory, expanded into advertising and went public. | The Portal Princess opens the city's map and sells shopfronts. |
| 1995–2000 | Cisco's routing and switching business expands into the internet buildout. | The Router Queen builds roads even rival city-states must travel. |
| 1999–2002 | Internet investment and telecom buildout crest then contract. | A festival of impossible capacity forecasts loses its lights. |
| 1999–2006 | Wireless messaging and mobile-device ecosystems vie for business and consumers. | Nokia and RIM contest a kingdom built around the pocket. |
| 2007–2012 | Touch-first smartphones and mobile platforms reset the competition; Nokia adopts Windows Phone in 2011. | The Empress learns the battle has shifted from handsets to applications. |
| 2009 | Nortel seeks CCAA court-supervised restructuring in Canada. | The Valkyrie disarms to help the network workers she once led. |

**Historical precision:** RIM is the corporation's name in the 1990s–2000s; BlackBerry was a product brand before the company's 2013 name transition. Avoid treating Nokia, Symbian and Windows Phone as the same entity. Nokia's Microsoft agreement was formalized in 2011. Nortel's January 2009 Canadian filing was a CCAA restructuring **at the time of filing**, not simply a bankruptcy.

## Setting and storytelling rules

1. **Networks are physical:** portal companies cannot route packets by will; router vendors cannot conjure fiber, radio spectrum or carrier contracts.
2. **The web is larger than Silicon Valley:** show Canada's telecom history, Nokia's Finnish roots, and Japanese mobile internet (NTT DoCoMo) when a chapter requires it.
3. **No logo-only characterization:** each silhouette and prop must be legible without branding. Company mark goes on a stable, era-correct removable accessory. Brand designs must pass separate approval.
4. **Respect employee experiences:** a company losing market position is not proof that its engineers were foolish. Use credible incentives, dependencies, timing and constraints.
5. **No retroactive births:** younger Season 1 heroines are the *same identity* in era-specific dress. No labs founded after the depicted year appear as active companies.
6. **Anachronism review:** date every referenced product, standard, acquisition, corporate identity and historical logo; make a timeline pass before illustrating.

## Five-arc structure

- **The City of Modems (1995–1999):** AOL opens access, Netscape navigates, Yahoo curates, Microsoft competes for the browser, Cisco and Nortel build the roads.
- **The Bubble City (1999–2002):** speculative capacity forecasts and real technical accomplishment collide with a financial bust.
- **The Pocket Kingdom (1999–2006):** Nokia's durable devices, RIM's push messaging, Palm's stylus and Japanese mobile services embody distinct paths.
- **The Touchscreen Coup (2007–2012):** young Apple and Android-era Google shift the battle to software ecosystems; Arm and Qualcomm expose hidden dependencies.
- **The Photo Album (present-day frame):** IBM recalls previous platform transitions; older Nvidia and Apple interpret the record, without altering current Season 1 identity.
