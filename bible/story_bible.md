# Silicon Wars: The Goddesses of Computation

A seinen sci-fi manga about anime heroines who personify technology companies. Think corporate warfare meets high-school rivalries: political intrigue, engineering competitions, and occasional comedy. Each heroine's powers derive from her company's real computing specialty. The central rule: even the strongest AI queen depends on compute, bandwidth, foundries, equipment and verification.

The heroines are not simply girls wearing corporate logos. Each one embodies her company's technology, history, competitive advantages, business philosophy, weaknesses, and relationships. Some are powerful queens, some ambitious underdogs, some brilliant inventors, and others indispensable specialists who quietly control the entire world.

The stories are fictional metaphors, not depictions of real employees or official product claims.

## Tone

The model is the intelligent dialogue and political maneuvering of *Spice and Wolf* or *The Apothecary Diaries*, combined with the technological spectacle of a mecha anime. The strongest scenes are those where two characters negotiate, argue, or expose one another's technological limitations, not simply where they fight. Comedy comes from character: old rivals bickering, a queen's dignity slipping, a grandmother's lecture at the dinner table.

## The world: the Great Compute War

The world has entered the Age of Artificial Intelligence. Computation has become the ultimate resource, and the great technology houses are racing toward the mythical creation known as AGI.

Nvidia controls the dominant dragon army of accelerated computing. AMD wants to break her monopoly. Intel seeks to restore her ancient empire. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and xAI compete to develop ever more powerful forms of intelligence.

But the real power lies deeper. ASML guards the technology of light. TSMC controls the art of turning designs into working silicon. Arm owns the architectural blueprints, Micron and the other memory houses provide memory, and Broadcom and Marvell ensure that the growing intelligence can communicate.

At first, everyone believes the story is about which AI heroine becomes the smartest. Then they discover a much more interesting problem: even infinite intelligence cannot escape the physical limits of computation, memory, energy, and manufacturing. That discovery makes room for both comedy and serious discussion of technology.

## Power system: interdependence

Power reflects technical interdependence, not market capitalization. Nvidia can defeat AMD in a battle and still need TSMC to manufacture her next weapon. TSMC requires ASML's equipment. OpenAI may create a revolutionary intelligence but remain dependent on cloud infrastructure and chips. No single heroine can conquer the world alone.

Every character profile names who she depends on and what her power cannot do.

## Artistic rules

1. **Every heroine needs a recognizable silhouette.** A reader should tell Nvidia's angular black leather and emerald circuit seams, Apple's clean geometry, TSMC's artisan uniform, and Hugging Face's yellow bows and mascot plush apart even with the company names removed.
2. **Every heroine needs a contradiction.** Intel is powerful but vulnerable. Nvidia is arrogant but genuinely brilliant. OpenAI is optimistic but impatient. Anthropic is careful but competitive. TSMC is quiet but extraordinarily influential. These tensions make characters believable.
3. **Power reflects technical interdependence,** as described above.

4. **Every heroine wears a company mark.** Integrate a recognizable company logo or wordmark into her outfit or a worn accessory, using a period version where appropriate. Keep that placement consistent across her production art.

## Two generations

- **The Modern Generation.** AI prodigies, semiconductor engineers, memory specialists, and corporate rivals fighting to control the future.
- **The Grandmother Generation.** Legendary women from computing's earlier eras. Some have retired, some are ghosts of vanished empires, and others are still active.

IBM and Oracle are the exception. They are not grannies. Each is an older, sophisticated, still-powerful woman, in her late 50s or early 60s, who has survived multiple technological revolutions. She has the authority of a former empress and absolutely no intention of retiring.

The younger characters occasionally call the older women Obaa-sama or Grandma, with reactions ranging from warm affection to murderous irritation.

## Character ages

- **Elementary-school child prodigies:** Mistral AI, DeepSeek, OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI. All designs and stories are wholesome and nonsexualized.
- **Middle-school students:** Hugging Face and Cerebras, about 12–13.
- **Mature executives:** IBM and Oracle, in their late 50s or early 60s. IBM is the silver-haired Iron Lady who survives every technological cycle.
- **Very old grandmothers:** Bell Labs (scholarly research grandmother), Fairchild (hands-on semiconductor matriarch).
- **Grandmothers:** DEC (burgundy canvas work jacket and khakis, informal minicomputer engineer around sixty) and Sun Microsystems (silver-haired Solar Archon in her late sixties or seventies, with half-moon glasses, a purple gown and a long charcoal coat).
- **Everyone else** is an adult. Meta is the youngest adult at 20; Microsoft and Apple are around 30.

## Core relationships

A heroine comes alive through someone who brings out her strengths and weaknesses. Each character profile in the [roster](../characters/INDEX.md) lists her relationships; these are the pillars of the season.

- **AMD vs Intel: the Hundred-Year Rivalry.** Intel acts like the former queen of the CPU kingdom. AMD is the rebellious challenger who refuses to remain in her shadow. Both were founded by people who left Fairchild, so their arguments are equal parts engineering debate and old-fashioned sibling rivalry, and Fairchild still yells at them for fighting at her table.
- **Nvidia vs AMD: the Dragon and the Challenger.** Nvidia has the throne, the infrastructure, and a fiercely loyal following. AMD believes she can win through clever design, openness, and competitive economics. Nvidia enjoys reminding her of the ecosystem advantage.
- **OpenAI vs Anthropic: the Estranged Scholars.** Former best friends who now sit apart at the Academy. OpenAI wants the class to try her prototype as soon as it runs. Anthropic asks how it works and whether they should test it first. They still know each other's arguments better than anyone.
- **TSMC vs Samsung vs Intel: the Three Foundry Houses.** Three powerful manufacturers, each with a different philosophy about how advanced chips should be built. TSMC is the disciplined specialist, Samsung the ambitious industrial empire, and Intel the historic integrated manufacturer fighting to reclaim manufacturing prestige.
- **ASML and TSMC: the Sorceress and the Artisan.** ASML builds tools that bend light to extraordinary precision. TSMC turns those tools and many other complex processes into mass-produced silicon. Their collaboration is central to the power of the advanced-chip kingdoms.

## Strict visual continuity

The approved art is canon. For a character with a model sheet, the sheet and reference card decide her look, and the text is corrected to match them. Characters without a model sheet are draft stand-ins; their text and cards may change when their sheets are made.

- Intel: long light-blue hair, pointed sapphire crown and a royal-blue imperial uniform with gold-fringed epaulettes. A small Intel Inside emblem sits near the hem of her left front skirt panel. Composed, authoritative pose.
- AMD: fiery red ponytail, red/black chiplet armor, athletic challenger stance.
- Nvidia, the Emerald Queen: thick emerald-green braid and a black leather jacket-dress with glowing green circuit seams. She has the greatest raw physical strength of the cast, with powerful arms and naturally proportioned athletic legs. Her CUDA Dragon appears as a green holographic dragon behind her when she uses her power; it is not part of her costume.
- Google: dark hair with rainbow streaks, white oversized G sweatshirt, Android plush and Waymo charm; no sunglasses or headphones.
- Microsoft: ash-blonde low bun, glasses in every view, charcoal business dress (never trousers) under a blazer, lanyard badge and windowpane mug.
- Apple: silver-gray bob, white lab coat over a black turtleneck, silver Apple pendant.
- Meta: blue-violet side bun, white VR goggles with the infinity emblem, black hoodie, violet holographic cape, and opaque striped stockings.
- Amazon: black smile-arrow cap, dark brown ponytail, black-and-orange work jacket, parcel and drone.
- Fairchild: rolled sleeves, red bandana, glasses, working forewoman; do not duplicate Bell Labs' silhouette.
- Bell Labs: gentle elderly scholar, tweed and vintage instruments.
- DEC: use her approved workshop portrait and model sheet. Sun: preserve the selected design from the final four-grandmothers infographic.

## Season 1 story threads

The [Season 1 character index](../characters/INDEX.md) defines the cast and each tier.

1. **Compute Triad.** Intel, AMD, and Nvidia anchor the season.
2. **Platforms.** Google, Microsoft, Apple, Meta, and Amazon are the first supporting group.
3. **AI labs.** Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Hugging Face, Mistral AI, and DeepSeek are the second supporting group.
4. **Foundry, equipment, and memory.** TSMC, ASML, Samsung, Micron, and SK hynix supply the manufacturing and memory threads.
5. **Other chip companies.** Arm, Qualcomm, Broadcom, Marvell, Huawei, and Cerebras round out the chip cast.
6. **Cameos.** IBM, Oracle, Sun Microsystems, Bell Labs, Fairchild Semiconductor, DEC, and Xerox PARC appear in smaller roles.
