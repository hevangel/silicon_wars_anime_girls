A 15-lesson art course for complete beginners. All images generated by Muse.

Think of each style as a visual language — a reusable library of marks, materials and conventions. The classification tags on every card say what kind of thing a style is (a medium, a movement, a cultural tradition, a technology…), and the filter below lets you browse by kind instead of by lesson. A style's grammar note names its characteristic moves, with the decisions it tends to use in Part II and the meanings it tends to carry in Part III — tendencies, never rules.

This is an art course built like a science experiment. In almost every picture, the same character — TSMC, the Quiet Queenmaker of Silicon Wars — sits in the same café pose: same chair, same cup, same crossed legs. Only one thing changes: the style. When everything else is held still, style itself becomes visible — and learning to see it is learning art history.

No experience needed

Written for college students who have never taken an art class. Every style gets a plain-language explanation and a short “what to look for” list.

Work through the lessons

Fifteen lessons walk from raw materials (pencil, ink, plaster) through world traditions, comics and modernism, ending inside video-game graphics. Each ends with a two-minute looking exercise.

Click any picture

Pictures open full-size. Styles with four variants are four executions of one idea — compare them; the differences are the lesson.

A note on the pictures: every image in this course is an AI-generated study of a fictional character, made to demonstrate a historical style. They are teaching illustrations, not works by the artists and cultures discussed — each style card links out so you can meet the real thing.
