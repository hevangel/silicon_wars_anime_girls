One style. One scene.
One change at a time.

A 15-lesson art course for complete beginners. All images generated by Muse.

The companion to the TSMC Style Course runs the experiment in reverse. There, the pose stayed fixed and the style changed. Here the style stays fixed — one clean, neutral digital painting — and so do the character and the café. Every card changes exactly one visual decision: where the light comes from, how warm the colors are, where the camera stands. The unchanged picture — the control, shown at right — sits beside every card, so the one change is the only thing you can see.

Compare, don't just look

Each card opens with the control, then its variants. Flip between them: whatever moves is the lesson.

Overlays are drawn live

Grids, arrows, rulers and swatches are drawn by this page on top of the pictures — never baked into them. Cards that need one have a “Show overlay” button.

Click any picture

Pictures open full-size, with the control first in line so you can step back and forth between before and after.

A note on the pictures: every image in this course is an AI-generated study of a fictional character, made to demonstrate one visual concept at a time. They are teaching illustrations. Lesson 15 is about the habits — sketchbook, critique, portfolio — that matter more, not less, when pictures are cheap to make.

The five strands

The lessons run in order, but they group into five conceptual strands: the vocabulary of marks and materials, how pictures represent the world, the grammar that arranges them, a first step into semantics — Lesson 14, the bridge to Part III — and the practice that ties it together.
