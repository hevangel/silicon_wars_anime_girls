# Pilot — The Portal, the Packet, and the Pocket

**Status:** Outline only; script, page YAML and renders await approval. **Format:** six panels in manga RTL reading order. **Window:** 1999, followed by a clearly captioned later epilogue.

| Panel | Camera / characters | Dialogue and technological stakes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Wide, an exaggerated Dial-Up Metropolis with winding cables and blinking routers; Yahoo stands on the portal balcony. | Yahoo: “Welcome! Everything is one click away!” The city is only reachable through fragile dial-up links. |
| 02 | Cisco routes luminous packets through three branching lanes while Nortel stands behind the fiber trunk. | Cisco: “One click needs a thousand working links.” Nortel: “And someone must lay them.” Show router versus physical optical transport as different powers. |
| 03 | Netscape and young Microsoft compete to control the browser gate. Yahoo watches the growing crowd. | Netscape: “A window onto the open web.” Microsoft: “Whose window?” Distinguish browser from website and network. |
| 04 | Match cut to a pocket-sized city. Nokia towers above rows of durable handsets. | Nokia: “The whole world can call.” A handset and carrier network remain coupled; no magic global service. |
| 05 | RIM/BlackBerry appears with secure keyboard bracers, sending a small guarded dispatch. | RIM: “But can the right message arrive now?” Show wireless data plus messaging servers. |
| 06 | A technical map tears across the screen with colored layers: physical link → route → portal → handset → software platform. | Yahoo: “So who owns the city?” Cisco: “Nobody. That's the problem.” Caption: “And the next war will fit in a pocket.” |
