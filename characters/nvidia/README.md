# Nvidia — The Emerald Queen

*Silicon Wars* fictional company personification. This fan-created character is not affiliated with the real company.

**Faction:** Silicon Kingdoms  
**Age portrayal:** adult  
**Hair:** emerald green  
**Signature color:** neon green

## Appearance
Long emerald-green braid, piercing emerald eyes, broad shoulders, strong arms, and naturally proportioned athletic legs. She has the greatest raw physical strength of the cast.

## Clothing and accessories
Black leather motorcycle jacket-dress with an angular collar, asymmetrical zipper, wide belt, heavy boots, and glowing green circuit seams; black goggles pushed up in her hair. The traces branch upward from the hem. Black goggles sit in her hair.

## Personality
Charismatic, ambitious, dominant and theatrical.

## How she speaks
Smooth, commanding and teasing; talks as though she already owns the table.

## How she thinks
Software ecosystems multiply the value of accelerated compute.

## Motivation
Define the AI computing platform and its software language.

## Flaw
Arrogance and dependence on suppliers and infrastructure.

## Rivals
AMD, Cerebras.

## Allies
TSMC, memory suppliers and AI labs.

## Signature power
**CUDA Dragon** — a fictional dramatization of the real company's technology.

## Signature dialogue
> Of course you are free to challenge me, darling. Just tell me which of my GPUs you're using to train your army.

## Character depth

### Look and presence
Beautiful but intimidating. Her emerald-green braid, black leather jacket-dress, and green circuit seams make a silhouette that reads as both biker and royalty. She has the greatest raw physical strength in the cast and handles steel beams like coat hangers. Other characters call her the Emerald Dragon Queen, because a coiled holographic green dragon appears behind her when she uses her power. The dragon is a power effect, not part of her costume.

### Core contradiction
**Arrogant but genuinely brilliant, and the strongest yet the most dependent.** She earned her throne with years of bets nobody else would make, and she knows it. She also cannot forge a single weapon, feed her dragon memory, or power her halls without other people. She graciously offers everyone a seat at her table while making sure they know who owns it.

### Personality in depth
Charismatic, confident, theatrical, and almost unbearably smug, with the stamina of someone who expects to be at the top for a long time. Under the swagger is a survivor who once nearly went under and has never fully relaxed since.

**How she talks.** Smooth, commanding, and teasing. She calls people "darling," makes generous offers, and phrases threats as hospitality. She is the kind of antagonist who offers you a seat at her table while making sure you know who owns the table. Her keynotes are performances, and so are her conversations.

**How she thinks.** She doesn't merely want to sell powerful hardware. She wants to define the entire computing ecosystem around it, from the chips and networks to the software and the language developers think in. In the story, CUDA is a magical language only her disciples understand.

### Background

| Real history | In the story |
|---|---|
| 1993 · Founded by three engineers who planned the company in a roadside diner booth | She was born in a diner booth and still orders the same breakfast when she needs to think |
| 1995–96 · The NV1 struggles; the company nearly fails | She nearly died young. Company lore says she is always "thirty days from going out of business," and she acts like it |
| 1997 · The RIVA 128 saves the company | Her first victory, won with the last of her strength |
| 1999 · GeForce 256, marketed as the world's first GPU | She named a whole category after herself. She has been framing the story ever since |
| 2000s · Gaming dominance; Team Green | Gamers kept her alive, and she remembers it |
| 2006–2007 · CUDA brings general-purpose computing to every GPU; years of seeding universities | She taught a language for a decade before the world needed it. Her disciples are everywhere |
| 2012 · AlexNet is trained on two of her gaming GPUs | The night the dragon woke |
| 2016 · The first DGX AI system is hand-delivered to OpenAI | She personally delivered the first throne to the AI Academy |
| 2020 · Acquired Mellanox networking; 2020–2022 bid for Arm fails under regulators | She bought the roads between the halls, then tried to buy Arm's blueprints and was refused |
| 2022 onward · Export controls restrict her most advanced chips in some markets | Even the queen's weapons are subject to the kingdoms' laws |
| 2023–2025 · The AI boom; in October 2025 she becomes the first company valued at $5 trillion | The throne of the Age of AI |
| Sept–Dec 2025 · Invests $5 billion in Intel for about 4 percent, with joint x86 products planned | She extends a hand to the old empress. Whether it is help or ownership depends on who you ask |
| Dec 2025 · A roughly $20 billion non-exclusive license of Groq's inference technology, hiring its leaders | She absorbs a rival's best ideas without buying the rival |
| 2025–2026 · Invests in AI labs that buy her chips, including Anthropic and OpenAI | She funds the disciples who buy her weapons, and the kingdoms ask whether the money runs in circles |
| 2026 · Vera Rubin enters full production; she halts China-bound H200 production and plans on no China data-center revenue | Her newest dragon wakes as a whole kingdom closes its gates to her |

### Company culture
- **"Speed of light"** · She measures every plan against the physical limit, not against last year. Anything slower than the limit is an excuse.
- **Flat and intense** · She has no patience for layers. She talks to everyone directly and expects everyone to keep up.
- **Long bets** · She funds ideas for a decade before they pay off and sneers at anyone who quits after a quarter.
- **Intellectual honesty** · She likes mistakes stated plainly and in public, so long as they aren't hers.

### Brand image
- **Neon green on black** · Team Green. The color of glowing seams in the dark.
- **The keynote** · Every appearance is a show with a reveal, a crowd, and a punchline.
- **Leather** · Her black leather jacket-dress is the company's keynote iconography worn as a uniform.
- **The GPU as a category** · She doesn't compete in markets; she names them.

### Relationships
- **AMD** · The Dragon and the Challenger. Nvidia has the throne, the infrastructure, and a fiercely loyal following. AMD believes she can win through clever design, openness, and competitive economics. Nvidia enjoys reminding her of the ecosystem advantage, and secretly enjoys having a rival who won't quit.
- **Intel** · The former queen. Nvidia's chips used to need Intel's processors as hosts; now Nvidia is the one investing. She treats Intel with exquisite courtesy, which Intel finds worse than contempt.
- **TSMC** · Her armorer. Every weapon Nvidia owns is forged in TSMC's halls and packaged on her advanced lines. Nvidia is never anything but gracious to TSMC.
- **SK hynix, Micron, and Samsung** · The memory houses whose HBM feeds her dragon. SK hynix is her favorite supplier and knows it.
- **The AI Academy (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI)** · Her disciples and customers. Several of them are quietly forging their own swords (TPUs, Trainium, custom chips) to escape her table, and she knows that too.
- **Cerebras** · A middle-school wafer engineer who wants to dethrone her. Nvidia pats her on the head, which drives Cerebras wild.
- **Arm** · The architect whose blueprints she once tried to buy. They work together politely.

### Dependencies and limits
- She cannot manufacture: TSMC forges her chips and packages them.
- Her dragon eats memory bandwidth. Without SK hynix, Micron, and Samsung, it starves.
- Her halls need power, cooling, and cloud operators to house them.
- Export rules decide where her weapons can go.
- Her customers' ambitions are her greatest risk: Google rents out TPUs, Amazon fields Trainium, Microsoft builds Maia, and AMD's Helios racks are shipping to her own customers.

### Private side
- **Deepest fear** · Going back to the diner booth, thirty days from the end. She never feels as safe as she looks.
- **Soft spot** · Gamers. She still remembers who kept her alive, and she feels a pang when they can't buy her cards.
- **Habits** · Lifts heavy things absent-mindedly, rehearses reveals in the mirror, and checks supply schedules at three in the morning.
- **Off duty** · Diner breakfasts, racing games, and pressing a dumbbell one-handed while answering messages.

### Voice samples
- Signature: "Of course you are free to challenge me, darling. Just tell me which of my GPUs you're using to train your army."
- Panel-length: "Challenge me if you wish. Which of my GPUs are you using?"
- Lifting the gate: "Need a hand?"
- Showing off: "I'm stronger than both of you together... with one hand."
- Unguarded, to TSMC: "Everything I am, you make real. Please don't tell anyone I said that."

**She would never** raise her voice, admit she is afraid, or let anyone see her wait for anything.

### Season 1 arc
She starts the season at the top, apparently untouchable. Pressure arrives not from rivals but from limits: TSMC's capacity, HBM supply, power, and export rules. Meanwhile her biggest customers forge their own swords. The open question is whether she can share the table, or whether owning it was always the point.

### Power in story
**CUDA Dragon** summons a coiled green holographic dragon written in a language only her disciples can read. It multiplies the strength of every GPU it touches and binds developers to her ecosystem. Its limit is hunger: the dragon needs constant feeding with memory, power, and fresh silicon from other people's halls.
