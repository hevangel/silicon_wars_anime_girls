## Appearance and clothing
Structured cobalt engineer's long coat over a practical routing-console harness; narrow teal light-pipe cords; period Cisco bridge mark on removable lapel badge. Her logo-free silhouette remains identifiable: High cable-loop crown and angular standing router-pack; asymmetrical sleeves shaped like forward arrows. Use a historically verified company mark on a consistent accessory; the mark is removable from the identity test.

## Personality and voice
Clipped, sardonic, economical; names paths, hops and failure domains. **Worldview:** A city belongs to whoever keeps its packets moving, not whoever owns its gate. **Motivation:** Build a network that works even when rulers disagree. **Contradiction:** she can be extraordinarily competent yet confuses indispensable infrastructure with an invulnerable business.

## Relationships and limits
- **Rivals:** Nortel (long-haul infrastructure), Juniper (precision routing).
- **Allies:** Sun and IBM as demanding enterprise customers; wary deals with Yahoo and AOL.
- **Dependencies:** Internet protocols, physical fiber and links, carriers who pay for capacity, deployed router software and network operators.
- **Power:** **Route Dominion**, an invented visual expression of the company's role.
- **Limit:** Cannot create bandwidth where no physical link exists or guarantee demand will justify buildouts.
- **Season 2 arc:** Starts as the indispensable builder of the city's streets; in the bust, confronts overcapacity and learns to govern by reliability rather than swagger.

## Performance samples
- **In command:** “Packets don't care who sits on the throne. Give me the next hop.”
- **Under pressure:** “Show me the failure mode, not another slogan.”
- **Private fear:** “If the world changes the rules, will they still need me?”
- **To an ally:** “I can do my part. I need you to do yours.”
- **Never says:** “My technology has no dependencies.”
