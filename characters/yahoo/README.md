## Appearance and clothing
Layered 1990s internet-cafe jacket, purple miniskirt over opaque tights, a portal-shaped folding fan, period Yahoo! badge. Her logo-free silhouette remains identifiable: Large portal ring fan, twin curled purple ponytail, bright layered cafe streetwear. Use a historically verified company mark on a consistent accessory; the mark is removable from the identity test.

## Personality and voice
Joyously fast, curious, interrupts herself with new destinations. **Worldview:** The web is a city; people need a welcoming front door, not just a list of addresses. **Motivation:** Make the internet legible, lively and useful for everyone. **Contradiction:** she can be extraordinarily competent yet chases so many services that her audience cannot tell what is central.

## Relationships and limits
- **Rivals:** AOL, Netscape and the younger search-focused Google.
- **Allies:** Content publishers, advertisers, online communities and network builders.
- **Dependencies:** The open web, network infrastructure, content and people willing to visit her portal.
- **Power:** **Portal Carnival**, an invented visual expression of the company's role.
- **Limit:** Cannot own the open web or force users to begin at her gate.
- **Season 2 arc:** From beloved guide to overwhelmed ruler: the pure search challenger reveals the limits of being everyone's homepage.

## Performance samples
- **In command:** “News, mail, games, finance—why choose one door when you can have a whole city?”
- **Under pressure:** “Show me the failure mode, not another slogan.”
- **Private fear:** “If the world changes the rules, will they still need me?”
- **To an ally:** “I can do my part. I need you to do yours.”
- **Never says:** “My technology has no dependencies.”
